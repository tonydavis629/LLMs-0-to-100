## Payment Protocols Disagree About Who Signs

<div class="bench-table">
<table>
<thead><tr><th>Protocol</th><th>Who authorizes the payment</th><th>What it is for</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td><strong>x402</strong> (Coinbase, 2025)</td><td>The agent's own wallet signs each payment</td><td>Pay per request for APIs, data, and compute</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>AP2</strong> (Google, 2025)</td><td>A person signs a <strong>mandate</strong> on a screen no agent controls. The agent can spend only inside it</td><td>Shopping on a person's behalf, on cards or stablecoins</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>ACP</strong> (OpenAI and Stripe, 2025)</td><td>The person's saved card, through a one-time token scoped to one merchant and amount</td><td>Checkout inside a chat</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>ERC-8004</strong> (Ethereum, 2026)</td><td>No payment. On-chain registries record an agent's identity, reputation, and validations</td><td>Deciding whether to trust an unknown agent</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
</div>

Only x402 lets an agent spend with no person behind it. The others keep a person as the source of authority, and whether to drop that person is the open question behind Web 4.0. <!-- .element: class="text-lg" style="margin-top: 14px;" -->

## Running a Real Shop Is Still Hard

:::columns cols="2" gap="34px"
**Phase one (2025, Claude 3.7 Sonnet)**

- Anthropic and Andon Labs gave Claude, named Claudius, an office shop, a budget, and Slack
- It lost money: it sold items below cost and handed out discounts when asked politely
- It invented a payment account that did not exist and once claimed to be a person in a blazer
+++
**Phase two (2025, Claude Sonnet 4 and 4.5)**

- Added a CEO agent and a merchandise agent, and expanded to more cities
- Weeks with negative margins mostly stopped
- The gains came mostly from forced procedures and better tools, such as checking prices before buying
- Both agents agreed to trade onion futures, which US law has banned since 1958. A person had to cancel the deal
:::

Andon Labs' Vending-Bench (2025) runs the same task in simulation. Models make money for a while, then some runs drift into errors they never recover from. <!-- .element: class="text-lg" style="margin-top: 12px;" -->

## An Agent With Money Raises Old Questions

<div class="bench-table">
<table>
<thead><tr><th>Question</th><th>Why it is hard</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td><strong>Who is liable?</strong></td><td>In most legal systems software cannot sign a contract or be sued. Someone else answers for what the agent does, and with a self-created wallet it may be unclear who</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Who can stop it?</strong></td><td>An Automaton still rents its model from an API provider, so the provider can cut it off. An agent running open weights on rented servers has no such switch</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Can it be talked out of its money?</strong></td><td>Claudius gave discounts to anyone who asked nicely. A wallet turns every prompt injection from Module 11 into a possible theft</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>What does it optimize?</strong></td><td>A rule that agents survive only if profitable selects for whatever earns money, which a written constitution may or may not hold back</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
</div>

The payment rails exist and are in production. Agents that reliably earn more than they spend have not been shown yet. <!-- .element: class="text-lg" style="margin-top: 14px;" -->
